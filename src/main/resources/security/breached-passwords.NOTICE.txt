  SHA-256 of the downloaded file: 424a3e03a17df0a2bc2b3ca749d81b04e79d59cb7aeec8876a5a3f308d0caf51
  The list is Mark Burnett's (Xato) "10 million passwords" set, the 1,000,000 most common entries.

How breached-passwords.txt was built from it
  1. Removed carriage returns.
  2. Kept only entries 12 to 128 characters long: the password policy already rejects anything
     shorter than 12 or longer than 128, so shorter entries could never match.
  3. Lower-cased every entry (the check is case-insensitive) and removed duplicates.
  4. Sorted (byte order), one entry per line, UTF-8, LF line endings.
  Result: 46,146 entries.

  Equivalent shell:
    tr -d '\r' < xato-net-10-million-passwords-1000000.txt \
      | awk 'length($0) >= 12 && length($0) <= 128' \
      | tr 'A-Z' 'a-z' | LC_ALL=C sort -u > breached-passwords.txt
